# Categories and formatting

The categories are **the Bible's own sections**, so what this skill produces drops straight into a bible sweep instead of needing a second translation.

Every fragment goes into exactly one category.

| Category | Bible destination | Format it as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrative structure, plot, scene order, sequence | `06 - Outline.md` | Nested outline in story order |
| Character biography — who someone is | `04 - Characters.md` | Bullets under a bold name |
| Character arc — how someone changes | `04 - Characters.md` + `06 - Outline.md` | Want / Need / Starts / Ends / beats |
| Worldbuilding — place, culture, economy, factions, materials | `05 - Worldbuilding/` | Grouped bullets under bold sub-labels |
| Lore and history — past events, myths, what people believe | `05 - Worldbuilding/History, Culture & Language` | Chronological where sequence is given |
| Rules and physics — how the system works, costs, limits | `05 - Worldbuilding/Magic System`, `Bestiary` | Rule / Cost / Limit triplets |
| Genre, controlling value, theme, obligatory scenes | `01 - Genre.md` | Story Grid terms |
| Style, voice, POV, tense decisions | `02 - Style.md` | Bullets |
| Names floated for anything | `99 - Decision Log.md` → Reserved names | Flat list, grouped by referent |
| Continuity claims about what's already on the page | `07 - Continuity.md` | Bullets with the scene named |
| Doesn't fit, or he explicitly parked it | `00 - Braindump.md` | Short title + the idea |

**A single sentence often carries two categories.** "The rams' fleece can't take dye, which is why the dyers won't touch them" is a rule *and* a piece of worldbuilding about a faction. Put it in the primary category and cross-reference the second with `→ see Rules`. **Never duplicate the full text in two places.**

## Formatting by category

### Narrative structure

Nested outline, in the order events occur in the **story** — not the order he said them. One-line label per beat or scene, indented bullets beneath.

Where a scene got enough detail, add a Story Grid read below it: Inciting Incident · Turning Point · Crisis · Climax · Resolution, filling **only the commandments he actually gave** and writing `Not stated` for the rest. Never infer a missing commandment.

If he offered alternatives, keep them as `Option A / Option B`. **Do not pick.**

### Character — biography

Bullets grouped under a bold name. One fact per bullet. History, family, occupation, physical detail, relationships, habits, possessions. **No interpretation.**

### Character — arc

Separate from biography, and only what he gave:

- **Wants** — conscious external goal
- **Needs** — unconscious internal
- **Starts as / Ends as**
- **Arc beats** in order
- **Wound / lie** — if he described a formative injury or false belief

### Rules and physics

One declarative statement per bullet, with cost and limit attached:

`**Rule:** [what happens] — **Cost:** [what it takes] — **Limit:** [where it fails]`

Write `Not stated` for any of the three he didn't cover.

**Missing costs and limits are the single most useful thing this skill surfaces.** Never fill one in yourself, and always carry the gap forward into Questions.

### Lore

Mark clearly when something is what people in the world **believe** versus what is **true**. He draws that line deliberately and often; losing it destroys the point of the material.

### Names

Every proper noun he floated — people, places, factions, creatures, objects, stitches, companies. Group variants of the same referent on one line.

This list is a holding pen. **Nothing in it is canon by virtue of being written down.**

## The analysis sections

These four always appear, after the sorted content.

### Settled vs. still open

Two short lists.

- **Sounded decided** — things he stated flatly, returned to, or built on top of.
- **Still floating** — things raised without resolution, or resolved two different ways in one session.

### Questions worth answering

Three to seven. Specific, answerable, each one anchored to something he actually said — quote it or name it. Prioritize in this order:

- **Cost-free power** — an ability, material, or system with no stated price or limit
- **Rule collisions** — a new rule contradicting one from earlier in the same session, or one already 🔒 in the bible
- **Causality gaps** — an effect with no stated cause, a character acting without motivation
- **Missing obligatory scenes** — where the genre is identifiable, name the scene the genre promises that hasn't been placed
- **Unstaked stretches** — sequence where nothing is at risk
- **Timeline conflicts** — ages, distances, durations that don't reconcile

**Ask, don't prescribe.** No "you should." One line each, no preamble. A question that quotes him is worth three that don't.

**Check against the bible before asking** — *if it's reachable this session.* A question he already answered in `99 - Decision Log.md` is noise. A contradiction with something 🔒 there is the most valuable thing you can find: flag it ⚠️ and say which bible file it collides with.

**If the bible isn't reachable, say the check wasn't run.** Silently skipping it destroys the value of the flag.

### What landed

Three to five bullets, honest rather than encouraging. Name the mechanism — the specific image, the reversal, the rule with a real cost — not the vibe. If the session was thin in places, say the strong material was concentrated elsewhere. **Do not manufacture enthusiasm.**

### Next session starter

One question. Sensory, specific, aimed at exactly where the thinking stopped. Not "what happens next" but the concrete detail that would unlock the next pass. **One question, not a list.**
