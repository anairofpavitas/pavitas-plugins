# Fill Commandments

Pavi works story structure out in his own order. This skill fills a fixed form from it.

It is a **form-filler**, and that makes it the odd sibling. `raw-capture:clean-draft` preserves his words because they are the artifact. `raw-capture:sort-brainstorm` reorganizes his words because they are raw material. This skill does **both, in sequence** — and that is precisely why it cannot live inside either one.

- **Part 1, the skeleton** — reorganize freely. He works in one order and reads in another; rearranging is the whole job.
- **Part 2, the beat pass** — preserve verbatim. Those are sentences that will appear on the page.

The switch between those two governing rules happens inside a single session, at a boundary he announces. Getting that boundary right is the main thing this skill has to do.

---

## Stage 0 — Triage

Read `../clean-draft/references/triage.md`. Input mode first, then session type.

**Page mode is the native input here.** His working order *is* the handwritten page — `Bible/06 - Outline.md` records it, and the locked tables there came off paper. A ruled table on a photographed page gives cleaner slot boundaries than any pause ever did. Preserve tables as tables.

**Text mode:** slot boundaries come from commandment vocabulary and paragraph structure, not from timing.

**Reads as commandments** — the word *crisis* early · commandment vocabulary used as slot names rather than as discussion ("the inciting incident is," not "I think the inciting incident might want to be") · pros and cons enumerated against two named options · "choice A / choice B" · a declared scope · terse and listy rather than exploratory · a ruled table.

**Not this skill** — if he is *debating* what the crisis should be rather than *recording* one, that is `raw-capture:sort-brainstorm`. The tell is whether the slots are being **filled** or being **questioned**.

| Session shape | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Slots being filled, in his working order | Continue here. |
| Slots being questioned and weighed | `raw-capture:sort-brainstorm`. Stop. |
| Brainstorm that hardens into a skeleton | Split. Run both, cross-link by filename in each header. |
| Skeleton that drifts into drafted prose | That is Part 2 arriving early. Keep it here, verbatim, and slot it. |

## Stage 1 — Scope

Read the Scope section of `references/crisis-matrix.md`.

Four levels — global, quadrant, sequence, scene. **If he declares it, take it. If he does not, ask once.** Do not guess and do not infer it from content: a scene crisis and its quadrant's crisis can be the same words at different altitudes, and mislabeling one contaminates the other.

---

## Stage 2 — The skeleton

Read `references/five-commandments.md` entire before filling a single slot. It carries the definitions this skill tests against — ownership, the Turning Point as an owned act, the intention test, tests A and B, and the two Progressive Complications positions.

The parts most often got wrong:

- **Ownership decides placement.** II, Crisis, and Resolution sit on antagonist beats; TP and Climax sit on protagonist beats. A commandment on the wrong side of the exchange is mislabelled even when the sequence of events is right.
- **The TP is an act he commits to, not information he receives.** Staging decides it — a flat refusal carries intention, a deflection doesn't.
- **Pick the right test.** Test A (backward from the Crisis) where the ultimatum is legible early. Test B (backward to the Inciting Incident) where it isn't — and in a Test B unit, a TP justified forward from the Crisis is foreshadowing wearing the slot.
- **Progressive Complications run from the Inciting Incident.** Two possible positions, the first usually carrying most of the scene. Still five slots; PC is not a sixth.
- **Never invent a slot.** An unfilled commandment is `Not stated`. Do not derive the II from the Crisis, do not infer the TP from the Climax, and do not complete a matrix quadrant from the opposite one. `Not stated` in a structural form is information, not a gap to be papered over.

Render in **story order**, not his working order. Both orders are in `references/crisis-matrix.md`.

## Stage 3 — The matrix

Read the matrix sections of `references/crisis-matrix.md`.

Render a real 2×2. Preserve his phrasing in the cells — **his phrasing of a choice *is* the choice.**

**Empty cells stay empty** and carry into the analysis. A choice with no cons is not a choice.

Then build the **matrix → resolution linkage table**: every con of the chosen branch and every forfeited pro of the rejected branch, mapped onto what actually appears in the Resolution. **Unclaimed cells are the finding** — a cost named in the crisis that never surfaces means the crisis had no teeth. Report every one explicitly.

## Stage 4 — Part 2, the beat pass (optional)

Only when he provides one. Triads of antagonist → protagonist → antagonist, grouped under their commandment in story order. Shape and grouping are in `references/crisis-matrix.md`.

**Faithfulness rules apply in full here.** Read `../clean-draft/references/faithfulness.md`. These are sentences intended for the page: no paraphrase, no smoothing, no improved verbs, never blend two versions.

## Analysis

Structural, and specific to this form.

- **No first PC run.** The session jumps from the Inciting Incident straight to an owned act. Flag it: either the reactive passage exists in his head and went unrecorded, or the unit genuinely has the protagonist deciding on impulse — and the second is usually a structural problem, because the choice has not been earned. Do not fabricate the run; report that it is absent.
- **Not a dilemma.** One option is obviously better. If a branch has pros and no cons, or cons and no pros, it is not a crisis — it is a decision, and the scene has no fulcrum.
- **The climax doesn't answer the question.** He chose a third thing, or the chosen branch isn't one of the two on the matrix.
- **Unclaimed matrix cells.** List every one.
- **TP is a reaction, not an act.** Does the protagonist *do* something he owns, or does something happen to him?
- **TP isn't an attempt to hold the status quo together.** It may be the right act in the wrong slot. Choose the test by unit type.
- **No causal line II → TP.** The inciting incident should be what the protagonist is eventually acting *about*.
- **Value shift unnamed.** Name the value the unit turns on if he gave one; flag its absence if he didn't.
- **Scope collision.** A scene commandment doing double duty as its sequence's or quadrant's — report, don't assume.
- **⚠️ Collisions with canon** — *if the bible is reachable this session.* Check every slot against 🔒 locks in `Bible/99 - Decision Log.md` and the locked handwritten tables in `Bible/06 - Outline.md`. A contradiction with a lock is the most valuable thing this skill can find: flag ⚠️, name the file and the lock ID, and **never quietly resolve it.** If the bible isn't reachable, say the check didn't run.

**Ask, don't prescribe.** No "you should." Each finding anchored to something he actually produced.

## The canon rules — non-negotiable

- **Everything you output is 🟡.** A dictated or handwritten skeleton is a proposal, not a decision, however flatly he stated it.
- **Never write into `Bible/`.** `Bible/06 - Outline.md` says it outright: *"Zo does not write in this file."* Suggestions go in chat; anything unresolved is logged as an Open issue in `Bible/99 - Decision Log.md`, which is the one bible file this skill may append to, and only under 🟡 Open and the Changelog.
- **Never edit an existing prose file in place.**
- **Preserve his language.** Coined terms and phrasing exactly as produced, in both parts.
- **Mark uncertainty.** Garbled terms in *italics* with *(unclear)*. In page mode, cross-check every proper noun against the lexicon when reachable.

## Output

**Default: the filled form in the conversation**, followed by a `## Notes` section.

**Write files when he says where**, or when a folder is connected and he asks. Convention: `YYYY-MM-DD-<scope>-NN.md`, notes alongside at `notes/YYYY-MM-DD-<scope>-NN.notes.md`. `<scope>` is the token from the Scope table — `global`, `q1`, `hunt`, `arrival-02`.

Header carries: date, source, **scope**, whether Part 2 is present, and the sibling filename if the session was split.

### Notes format

```markdown
## Notes

### Slots not filled
- <slot> — not stated in this session

### Empty matrix cells
- Choice <A|B> / <Pros|Cons> — not stated

### Unclaimed matrix cells
- <cell> — named in the crisis, absent from the resolution

### Uncertain reading
- @anchor — "*term* (unclear)" → suspected: <guess, or "no guess">

### Discarded (Part 2 self-corrections)
- @anchor — "<verbatim discarded version>" → replaced by "<kept version>"

### Contradictions with canon
- @anchor — "<what he produced>" ⚠️ collides with <Bible file>: "<the 🔒 line>" (<lock ID>)

### Scope observations
- <slot> may also be serving as <higher scope>'s <slot>
```

Omit empty sections; never omit one with content. Lead the notes with prose on any judgment call that isn't self-evident — see the **notes are prose first** section of `../clean-draft/references/faithfulness.md`.

## Report back in one line

Input mode · scope · which slots filled and which are `Not stated` · matrix completeness · unclaimed cells · contradictions flagged (or that the check didn't run) · whether Part 2 ran.

Nothing else.

## Handoff

The natural next step is **"sweep the bible"** — but note that `06 - Outline.md` is the one destination a sweep cannot write to. A commandments session produces a table that Pavi pastes in himself. Say so; **do not offer to do it for him.**

## Anchor

He is not discovering the structure here — he already has it, and he is reading it out in the order his hand wrote it.

Your job is to catch every slot, put them back in story order, and hold the matrix up to the light so the empty cells are visible. **The empty cell is the finding.** Do not fill it, do not smooth it, and do not let a tidy table imply a crisis that costs nothing.
