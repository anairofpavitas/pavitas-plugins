# Fiction formatting

Standard English fiction convention, markdown output. Read by `clean-draft`, and by `fill-commandments` for its Part 2 beat pass.

Formatting sits **last** in the priority order. It never justifies changing a word.

---

## Prose

- **Dialogue:** double quotes. Comma inside the quote before a speech tag. New paragraph per speaker.
  `"I won't," she said.`
- **Interruption:** em dash inside the quote. `"But I—"`
- **Trailing off:** ellipsis. `"I thought maybe…"`
- **Paragraphs:** blank-line separated. No indentation.
- **Scene break:** `* * *` on its own line.
- **Chapter:** `## Chapter N` — **only when he says so.**
- **Internal thought:** italics, only where he signals it. Don't infer.

## Spoken and written punctuation

If he says or writes "period," "comma," "new paragraph," "quote unquote," honor it as punctuation and remove the word — **unless it's obviously prose**: "she put a period at the end of the argument" stays.

## Numbers

Follow fiction convention: spell out under 100 in narrative prose, numerals in LitRPG system output.

## Line wrapping

One paragraph is one unbroken line. The blank line is the only structural newline. Hard-wrapped paragraphs show a visible break at every wrap point in Obsidian and other rich editors.

---

## LitRPG

System messages, stat blocks, notifications, and skill readouts are dictated or written conversationally and must be formatted properly — fenced code block or blockquote, **consistent within the project**.

**Check how prior chapters in the project do it and match.** Don't invent a house style.

**Preserve his exact stat names and values. Never normalize or "fix" a number.** A stat line that looks wrong is a finding for the notes, not something to correct.

---

## Match the project, not this file

Where the project's existing files already establish a convention — dialogue punctuation, how system blocks are set, how scene breaks are marked — **the project wins.** Read one recent file before writing if you can reach it. This file is the fallback when you can't.
